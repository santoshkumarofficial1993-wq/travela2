Nine people, eight countries, one office above a bakery

Firstlight has been running small-group trips out of Seattle since 2016. We are not a marketplace, an aggregator or a booking engine. We are the company that answers the phone when your connection is cancelled.

Why the company exists

In 2015 our founder took a well-reviewed, expensive group tour through southern Spain. The bus held forty-four people. Three of the eight days included a stop at a shop that paid the guide a commission. The hotel on night five was a highway property nine miles from the town on the brochure cover.

None of that was a scam. It was all technically as described. It was simply what happens when a trip is designed to be sold rather than designed to be taken. Firstlight started the following year as an attempt at the other thing, and the constraints we set then are the ones we still work under.

Four rules we have not broken yet

These are operating constraints, not values statements. Each one costs us money, which is the only reason they are worth printing.

01

Twelve travelers, hard cap

We have turned away full-price bookings on sold-out departures rather than add a thirteenth seat. Group size is the single biggest determinant of what a trip feels like.

02

No commission from anyone local

Not from shops, restaurants, workshops or hotels. Our guides are on a day rate. If we take you somewhere, the only reason is that it is worth going.

03

A region needs a resident

We will not open a country until someone on the team has lived and worked there. It is why the list is eight long after nine years instead of eighty.

04

Say no out loud

If the month is wrong, the pace is wrong for your knees, or another operator does it better, you will hear that on the first call rather than in a review afterwards.

The people you will actually deal with

Four of the nine. The rest are resident guides who work our departures in their own countries.
