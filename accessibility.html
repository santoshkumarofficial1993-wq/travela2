Accessibility statement

Two separate things: how usable this website is, and how usable our trips are. We would rather be specific about both than claim everything is fine.

Last reviewed: 1 January 2026.

1. This website

We build to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2 at Level AA as our target. Specifically, this site is built with:

  • Semantic HTML landmarks, a single H1 per page, and a heading order that does not skip levels.
  • A visible skip link to the main content, reachable with the first Tab press.
  • Keyboard operation throughout, including the mobile menu, the filter controls, the accordions and the cookie banner. Escape closes the menu and returns focus to the button that opened it.
  • Visible focus indicators that we have deliberately not removed.
  • Body text contrast of at least 4.5:1, and large text at least 3:1, against its background.
  • Touch targets of at least 44 by 44 pixels on interactive elements.
  • Text alternatives on meaningful images, and empty alt attributes on images that are purely decorative so screen readers skip them.
  • Form labels that are always visible, never placeholder-only, with errors announced next to the field they belong to.
  • Full support for prefers-reduced-motion. Every animation on the site collapses to a static state when your system asks for reduced motion.
  • Layout that reflows to a single column without horizontal scrolling down to 320 pixels wide, and that survives 200% text zoom.

Where we currently fall short

Publishing the gaps is more useful than claiming there are none:

  • The embedded map on the contact page is a third-party component and is not fully keyboard navigable. The full postal address is published as text directly beside it, and directions are available by phone.
  • Third-party advertising units on guide pages are outside our control and their accessibility varies by advertiser.
  • Some destination photographs carry short alt text that describes the scene but does not attempt to capture every detail.
  • We have not yet published text transcripts for the audio briefings we send to booked travelers. Transcripts are available on request today, and will be standard.

How we test

Keyboard-only passes on every template, automated checks in the build, and manual screen reader testing with VoiceOver on macOS and iOS and NVDA on Windows. We have not yet commissioned an independent third-party audit; when we do, we will publish the result including the parts we failed.

2. Our trips

We do not claim our trips are universally accessible, because they are not. What we can promise is an honest description before you pay.

  • Every trip page states the activity level and the hardest single day, in miles and terrain rather than in adjectives.
  • Several routes use historic guesthouses with stairs and no elevator. We name the property in advance so you can check it yourself.
  • Parts of the Norway and Peru itineraries have no vehicle access and cannot be adapted for wheelchair users.
  • The Portugal and Utah trips can usually be adapted for travelers with limited mobility, including alternative activities on walking days and step-free lodging, arranged in advance.
  • Dietary requirements, including allergies, coeliac disease, vegetarian and vegan, are accommodated on every trip when told at booking.
  • Travelers who are Deaf or hard of hearing are welcome. We provide written daily briefings as standard and will work with an interpreter you bring.
  • Travelers who are blind or have low vision are welcome, and we will discuss guide-to-traveler ratio for the specific route.
  • A necessary companion or personal assistant travels at our cost price with no operating margin.

Call us and describe what you need. The worst outcome is that we tell you a particular route will not work and suggest one that will.

3. Alternative formats

Any page on this site, and any itinerary or booking document, is available in large print, as plain text, or read aloud over the phone. Ask and we will send it, at no charge, usually within two business days.

4. Tell us about a barrier

If something on this site or on a trip was harder than it should have been, we want the detail. Tell us the page or the moment, what you were trying to do, and what got in the way.

5. Legal context

We aim to meet our obligations under the Americans with Disabilities Act and under Washington State law, and we treat WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the practical standard for the website. This statement describes our current position honestly rather than asserting full conformance.
